About Her

Wangari Maathai was born on 1st April 1940 in Ihithe village, Nyeri, Kenya. She grew up close to the land, fetching water from a stream near her home and watching the forests around her. Those early memories stayed with her for the rest of her life.

She went on to become a scientist, a university professor, a Member of Parliament and, in 2004, the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize. She passed away on 25th September 2011 in Nairobi, but the trees she planted are still growing.

Her Journey

Education

She studied Biology in the United States at Mount St. Scholastica College and later earned a Master's degree from the University of Pittsburgh. In 1971 she received her PhD from the University of Nairobi, becoming the first woman in East and Central Africa to earn a doctorate.

The Green Belt Movement

In 1977 she started the Green Belt Movement. The idea was simple: pay rural women a small amount to plant trees. This helped bring back firewood, clean water and good soil, and it gave the women income and confidence.

Standing up for what is right

She was not afraid to speak against powerful people. In 1989 she opposed the plan to build a large tower in Uhuru Park, and later she fought to save Karura Forest from land grabbers. She was arrested and beaten several times, but she never gave up.

  • First African woman to receive the Nobel Peace Prize (2004)
  • First woman in East and Central Africa to earn a PhD
  • Served as an MP and Assistant Minister for Environment
  • Inspired tree-planting campaigns across Africa and the world

Why I Admire Her

What I admire most about Wangari Maathai is that she started small. She did not wait for a big budget or for the government to act. She picked up a seedling and planted it, and then she taught others to do the same. That shows me that real change can begin with one person.

She was also brave. Many people would have stopped after being arrested or insulted, but she kept going because she believed the land and the people mattered. As a student, she reminds me to finish what I start, even when it gets difficult.

“It's the little things citizens do. That's what will make the difference. My little thing is planting trees.”
— Wangari Maathai
